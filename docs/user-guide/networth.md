# Net worth

Everything you own and owe, not only what is invested: a flat, a car, valuables, money held
somewhere the app has no ledger for, and debts such as a mortgage, a loan or a credit card.

**Net worth is a second total, not a new value for your portfolio.** Every other screen keeps
measuring the invested part alone. Nothing entered here appears in returns, risk, allocation or
rebalancing, and nothing here has a return of its own — a figure you typed twice, a year apart, is
not a performance. The screen shows both totals side by side for that reason: *invested* is the
portfolio, *net worth* is invested plus what else is owned minus what is owed.

**Net worth ignores the account picker.** These things are not accounts, so no lens narrows them;
the screen reads the whole portfolio wherever you happen to be looking.

## Owned, owed, and the figure in force

Add something with **Add asset** or **Add debt**. A debt is entered as what is *still owed*, as a
positive amount — the subtraction is the screen's job, so a minus sign here would only be a typo.

Nothing on this screen is priced by a market. Each row carries dated valuations you write
yourself, and the figure in force on any day is the last one dated on or before it. So the line
steps rather than curves: between two valuations nothing was measured, and the app will not invent
the days in between. Every row names the day its figure is from, which is the only honest way to
show a number whose age matters.

A thing is absent before its first valuation — the flat you bought in March is not in February's
net worth — and absent again from the day set in **Closed on**, which is how a sold flat or a
repaid loan leaves without deleting its history. Something you have added but never valued is
listed as waiting for its first figure rather than counted as zero.

**Update value** adds a valuation for a day; the same day answered twice replaces that day's
figure, so correcting a typo does not leave two truths. **Value history** lists every figure ever
written for one thing and lets you remove one.

## Debts

**Interest rate** and **Monthly payment** describe a debt and change no balance: what is owed
today is whatever you last valued it at. They answer when the debt ends and what it still costs,
looking forward only. A measured balance and a modelled one that disagreed would make the figure
worth nothing.

**Secured by** links a debt to the thing it financed — a mortgage to its flat. The link shows the
relationship and moves no number: your monthly payment is already recorded as a withdrawal or a
charge on some account, and deducting it from the debt as well would count the same money twice.

## Account, asset or instrument

Which one something is depends on how you keep it, not on what it is.

- You record operations against it — deposits, interest, transfers? It is an **account**, and
  contribution limits can apply to it.
- You only restate it as one figure now and then? It belongs **here**, and no limit can see it.
- It has a price somebody publishes — a listed fund, a share, a crypto coin? It is an
  **instrument**, and it belongs in the portfolio, where it can have a return.

Margin debt at a broker is the one debt that does not belong here: it is negative cash on the
account and part of the invested capital, so leaving it in the portfolio is what keeps returns
right.
