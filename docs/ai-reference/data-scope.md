# Data scope (the account lens)

The user picks which accounts they are looking at. Everything reported — value, return, income,
allocation — is answered through that lens. It is a point of view, not a filter on the ledger.

Every transaction has two sides: the instrument moves on a securities account, the money moves on a
deposit account. When a scope contains only one of the two, the other side is rewritten as money
crossing the portfolio boundary — a purchase seen from the securities account alone becomes an
instrument arriving from outside; seen from the deposit account alone, a withdrawal.

The consequence matters and surprises people: **a securities account on its own shows price
performance only.** Dividends, interest and fees settle on the deposit account, so from a
depot-only lens they are outside the picture and the return is the price movement alone.

Trading is the exception to that rewrite, and deliberately so. A purchase seen from the securities
account alone is shown as an instrument arriving, but it was still this portfolio buying: turnover
counts it, and the commission it cost is still reported as a cost. Only instruments that genuinely
arrived from elsewhere — transferred in from another broker — count as neither. Seen from the
deposit account alone the reverse holds: the money left, no instrument arrived, and that lens has
traded nothing.

Some things deliberately ignore the lens, because they are facts about an instrument or about the
whole portfolio rather than about the accounts in view:

- an instrument's dividend history and payment schedule,
- price alerts and instrument events,
- investment plans,
- watchlists.

Narrowing the picker must not make a quarterly payer look like it has never paid, and must not hide
next month's savings plan.
